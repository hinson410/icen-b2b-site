Operating Room

Operating Room Equipment Solutions

ICEN supplies operating room equipment from Mindray - anesthesia systems including the WATO EX-35, A5, A7 and A9, patient monitoring systems including the BeneVision N1, N12, N17 and N19, ePM 10, ePM 12, ePM 12M and ePM 15, uMEC 10, and VS 8 and VS 9) and BeneFusion infusion devices including the eVP, nVP, eSP, nSP, eDS and nDS) - and coordinates wider theatre equipment on a project basis for hospitals in 150 countries.

Anesthesia systems

The anesthesia range covers the WATO EX-35 for routine surgical lists, the A5 for mid-acuity operating rooms, and the A7 and A9 for high-acuity and teaching-hospital requirements. The A7 and A9 use an 18.5-inch capacitive touchscreen with 360-degree rotation, which matters in theatres where the machine is operated from more than one side of the table.

Anesthesia specification is driven by patient mix, case mix and how the theatre will be used. A day-surgery list and a teaching hospital performing complex cases do not need the same platform, and the difference is mostly in monitoring integration, ventilation modes and the ability to log and review cases.

Monitoring and infusion in theatre

The same monitoring platforms used in ICU cover theatre. The BeneVision N1 works as a standalone monitor, as an N-Series module, or as a transport monitor, so monitoring continues when a patient moves between theatre, recovery and ICU. The BeneFusion range adds volumetric infusion pumps (eVP and nVP), syringe pumps (eSP and nSP) and infusion supervision systems (eDS and nDS).

Because these devices share consumables and staff training with the rest of the hospital, theatres are usually specified together with ICU and the wards rather than in isolation.

How to specify theatre equipment

Case mix and patient population

Adult, paediatric and neonatal capability, and whether the theatre handles routine or complex cases.

Monitoring integration

Whether the anesthesia system and the patient monitor are specified to work as one station or as separate devices.

Theatre layout

Machine position relative to the table, screen orientation and access for the anesthesia team.

Gas supply and installation

Medical gas, scavenging and electrical requirements should be reviewed before delivery, not during commissioning.

Accessories and consumables

Breathing circuits, masks, filters and cannulae belong in the same quotation as the capital equipment.

Training and service

Agree who trains the anesthesia team and who owns the service route.
