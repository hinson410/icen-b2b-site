Product Overview

ICU-level ventilation in a 6.5 kg transport ventilator

The Mindray TV80 is a transport ventilator that delivers ICU-level ventilation for all types of patients, from newborns to adults, covering invasive ventilation, non-invasive ventilation and oxygen therapy. Its 6.5 kg body and hot-swappable battery address the portability and runtime requirements of a transport ventilator.

Battery operation is specified at >=11 hours, and the real-time battery display stays readable even when the unit is powered off, so remaining capacity can be checked before a transfer. Peak flow of >=260 L/min supports more effective non-invasive ventilation, and FiO2 is precisely adjustable from 21% to 100% using a non-consuming O2 sensor.

A dedicated proximal flow sensor measures tidal volume precisely with a minimum of 2 mL, which matters for neonatal and small paediatric patients. The TV80 also supports plug-and-play modular integration with a patient monitor and bedside-ventilator setting synchronization, with real-time O2 consumption monitoring. ICEN supplies the TV80 to hospitals, critical-care units and distributors, and confirms configuration, documentation and destination-market requirements before quotation.

FAQ

Common questions about Mindray TV80 Transport Ventilator

What patient range does the Mindray TV80 support?

The TV80 covers all types of patients from newborns to adults, delivering ICU-level ventilation including invasive ventilation, non-invasive ventilation and oxygen therapy.

How long can the TV80 operate on battery?

The hot-swappable battery is specified with an operating time of >=11 hours, and the real-time battery display remains readable even when the unit is powered off.

Does the TV80 support non-invasive ventilation?

Yes. Peak flow of >=260 L/min is specified to support more effective non-invasive ventilation (NIV), alongside invasive ventilation and oxygen therapy.

How precisely can FiO2 be controlled?

FiO2 is precisely adjustable from 21% to 100% using a non-consuming O2 sensor, so oxygen concentration does not depend on a consumable sensor element.
