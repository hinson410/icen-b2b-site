What the table shows

Four things buyers usually get wrong

1. Neonatal capability is not a flagship feature

The A1 reaches a 10 ml minimum tidal volume and the A3 reaches 10 ml, or 5 ml in PCV-VG – the same numbers as the A9. The A5 and A7 start at 20 ml in volume modes. If neonatal ventilation is the reason a department is looking upward through the range, the answer is not automatically the most expensive machine on this list. Read the tidal volume column before the price column.

2. The WATO EX-30 stops at children

Mindray publishes the EX-30 patient range as "children to adults". It does not cover neonates. This is the single most common misreading in this category, because the EX-30 sits next to the EX-35, whose published range is adult, paediatric and infant, and beside the A Series machines that all include neonates. A theatre mix that includes neonatal cases rules the EX-30 out regardless of budget.

3. A5 and A7 share the same ventilation platform

Both publish the same seven named modes plus optional SIMV-VG, the same tidal volume windows, the same 90 and 180 minute battery figures and the same configured mass of roughly 140 kg. The step from A5 to A7 is bought in the interface – an 18.5 inch rotating screen against 15.6 inch – and in the electronic flowmeter on the A7. That is a real difference for a teaching theatre with several people watching one screen. It is not a difference in ventilatory capability, and it should not be sold as one.

4. Half the range does not publish a tidal volume figure

The EX-30 and the EX-65/EX-55 have no published minimum tidal volume and no published patient range in Mindray's public material. That is not a reason to avoid them – both are mainstream machines – but it does change the procurement step: for these two, the datasheet request is compulsory, not optional. A quotation that does not include the specification annex is not comparable with one that does.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I compare these machines on price alone?

Not usefully. A quotation can differ by configuration – vaporizers, gas modules, monitoring, trolley – before the base machine is priced. Compare quotations line by line against the field that matters to your case mix, which for most buyers is patient range and minimum tidal volume.

Why do some cells say "not published"?

Because the manufacturer's public page for that model does not state the figure. ICEN does not fill those gaps by inference from a sibling model. Where the value matters to your decision, we request the official datasheet for that model before quoting.

Does a bigger screen mean better ventilation?

No. On this list the A5 and A7 share an identical ventilation platform and differ mainly in screen size and flow measurement. Screen size reflects the monitoring and teaching workflow, not the ventilator.

Can ICEN supply the specification annex with a quotation?

Yes. For models where the public data is thin, the quotation should be read together with the manufacturer's specification document. Ask for it explicitly and it travels with the quote.
