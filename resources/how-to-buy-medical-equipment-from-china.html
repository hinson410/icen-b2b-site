Buying Guide

How to Buy Medical Equipment from China

A practical guide for international buyers sourcing medical equipment from China: product selection, documentation, quotation, shipping and after-sales support.

Buying Guide

1. Define the project

Most problems in an international medical equipment purchase trace back to an unclear project definition. Suppliers cannot quote a scope they have not been given, and quotations built on different assumptions cannot be compared.

Write the project definition down before contacting anyone. It should cover the country and region of installation, the clinical departments involved, the intended use of each item, the quantities, the budget range you are working within, and the delivery window you need. Where a project is funded by a tender or a development programme, include the specification format that the funder requires, because it often dictates how the quotation must be structured.

Project definition fields and why each matters
FieldWhat to recordWhy it matters
Destination country and regionWhere the equipment will be installed and usedRegistration, market authorisation and documentation requirements are country-specific
Clinical departmentsThe departments the equipment is forDetermines configuration, accessories and consumables for each item
Intended useThe clinical purpose of each itemPrevents substituting a different tier of device than the one required
QuantitiesNumber of units per itemDrives packing, shipping method and consolidation decisions
Budget rangeThe range within which the project must landAllows a supplier to propose a realistic configuration instead of a theoretical one
Delivery windowWhen the equipment must be on siteDetermines whether sea, air or consolidated freight is workable
Funder or tender formatAny required specification or quotation formatA quotation in the wrong format may be rejected regardless of price

One field is frequently missed and worth adding: who will operate and maintain the equipment after installation. If the receiving hospital has no prior experience with the platform, the training and service requirements belong in the project definition from the start, not in a separate conversation after the equipment arrives.

2. Build a precise equipment list

An equipment list is the document the whole purchase runs on. Its purpose is to make every supplier quote exactly the same scope, so that differences in price reflect real differences in offering rather than differences in what was assumed.

Use fixed fields for every line, so that a comparison can be done column by column. The fields that cause the most disputes when omitted are configuration, included accessories and consumables — a monitor quoted without its parameter modules, or an analyzer quoted without reagents, is not the same product as one quoted with them.

Recommended fields for a medical equipment list
FieldExample of what to write
ManufacturerThe brand, for example Mindray or Edan
ModelThe exact model designation, including any series or suffix
ConfigurationScreen size, modules, parameters or capability level required
AccessoriesProbes, cables, cuffs, sensors, mounts and other included items
ConsumablesReagents, controls or single-use items needed to operate the device
QuantityUnits required per line
DocumentationManuals and technical documents required for your market
InstallationWhether installation is required and who performs it
TrainingWho is trained, on what, and where
WarrantyThe warranty terms you require to be quoted
ShippingDelivery terms, packing requirements and the receiving address

As an illustration of why model precision matters, ICEN lists 12 Mindray ultrasound systems, 11 patient monitoring models, six ventilators, four anesthesia systems, seven hematology analyzers, seven clinical laboratory systems, five digital radiography systems, six infusion devices, eight cardiac care devices and 17 veterinary systems. Several of those categories contain models that differ only in differential capability, screen size or throughput — so a line that reads simply "patient monitor" or "hematology analyzer" gives the supplier no way to quote accurately.

3. Check documentation

Documentation is where international purchases most often stall, and it is usually cheaper to settle it before the order than after the goods are manufactured.

Establish, in writing, which documents you require and who is responsible for each. In practice this means distinguishing between the documents that travel with the product, the documents your customs authority requires, and the documents your health regulator requires before the equipment can be used clinically.

Documentation categories to confirm before ordering
CategoryWhat to confirm
Product documentsOperator manual and technical documentation for each model in the order
CertificatesWhich certificates apply to the specific model and configuration being purchased
Market authorisationThe registration status required in your destination country, and who handles it
Customs documentsThe paperwork your import authority requires, and the exact consignee details
LanguageWhether manuals and labelling are required in a specific language for your market
TraceabilitySerial numbers or identifiers linking delivered units to documentation

The general rule is consistent across markets: product availability, registration and market authorisation depend on the destination market, and they vary by country. Confirm the specific requirements that apply to your country with ICEN before ordering, rather than assuming that a document set accepted elsewhere will be accepted locally.

4. Compare more than unit price

A quotation comparison that only reads the unit price will usually select the wrong supplier. Two offers for the same model at different prices are often offers of different scopes, and the cheaper one frequently excludes an item that then has to be purchased separately at a worse price.

Build the comparison so that every column is a total rather than a rate. Landing cost, accessories, consumables, installation, training and warranty all belong in the same table.

Factors to compare between supplier offers
FactorWhat to compare
Scope completenessWhether the offer includes the full configuration, accessories and consumables list
WarrantyThe terms offered and what is covered within them
ServiceWho provides service in your country, and through what route
Spare partsAvailability and ordering path for commonly replaced parts
DocumentationWhether the document set you require is included or treated as an extra
Lead timeThe stated manufacturing and delivery time for the full order
Export capabilityExperience shipping to your market, and how packing and consolidation are handled
TrainingWho is trained, on what equipment, and whether it is on site or remote

Two structural questions are worth asking of every supplier, because they reveal how much of the project risk sits with you. The first is what happens if one item on the list is unavailable at the agreed time — whether the order ships incomplete or waits. The second is who owns the interface between the supplier and your local service provider once the equipment is installed. Neither is a price question, and both routinely decide whether a project runs smoothly.

5. Confirm logistics

Logistics decisions are driven by the equipment list, not the other way round. A hospital order typically mixes large imaging systems with small bench instruments and fragile accessories, so packing and consolidation usually matter as much as the choice of shipping method.

Settle the following before the goods are packed. Delivery terms and who is responsible at each stage of the journey. Whether the order ships as a single consolidated consignment or in separate shipments. The packing standard required for the most fragile item in the order. The documentation that must accompany the shipment, and the exact consignee and notify-party details. And the receiving arrangements at destination, including who handles customs clearance and unloading.

Logistics points to agree before shipping
PointWhat to agree
Delivery termsWhich terms apply to the order and where responsibility transfers
ConsolidationWhether the order ships complete or in instalments, and the consequence of a split shipment
PackingThe standard and marking required, particularly for imaging and laboratory equipment
Shipping documentsThe papers that must travel with the goods, and to whom they are issued
Consignee detailsExact consignee, notify party and delivery address, checked character by character
ClearanceWho is responsible for customs clearance and any import duties at destination
Delivery to siteWhether delivery is to the port, the city or the installation room

Consignee details deserve a specific warning, because they are the single most common cause of avoidable delay in international medical equipment shipping. The name, address and contact details on the shipping documents must match what your customs authority expects, and they must match across every document in the set. Check them against the import paperwork before the goods leave, not after.

6. Keep after-sales expectations clear

After-sales arrangements are easier to agree before the order than to negotiate afterwards, because at that point both sides have something to lose from delay. Write them into the quotation rather than leaving them to be settled informally.

Confirm installation support and who performs the installation. Confirm the training scope: who is trained, on which models, in what language, and whether it happens on site or remotely. Confirm the technical contact for service questions, and the route by which a service request is raised. Confirm how warranty claims are handled, including who diagnoses the fault and who supplies the replacement part. Confirm the spare-parts list for commonly replaced items and the ordering path for each. And confirm who supplies consumables and quality-control materials on an ongoing basis.

After-sales items to document in the quotation
AreaConfirm in writing
InstallationWho performs it, what is included, and what site preparation is the buyer's responsibility
TrainingParticipants, models covered, language and delivery method
Technical contactThe named contact and channel for service questions
Warranty handlingHow a claim is raised, diagnosed and resolved
Spare partsThe common replacement items and how they are ordered
ConsumablesThe ongoing supply route for reagents, controls and single-use items
Software and updatesWhether updates are included and how they are applied
